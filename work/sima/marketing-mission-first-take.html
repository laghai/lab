The rule this whole memo reduces to

Both initiatives are audience-modelling problems wearing marketing clothes — and both stall on the same missing asset: CITYarts' own donor and reach data, which nobody has confirmed exists.

She asked for campaign concepts and a CRM programme. Neither can be specified before the four audiences have defined targeting characteristics, and the characteristics come from records only CITYarts holds. She conceded this herself at 12:57 — "I don't think they collect any data right now" — and again at 13:17 about the 200,000 figure. The fastest useful move is therefore not a campaign concept. It is a data request.

What I would propose she aims at

You pressed twice for a target she could hit inside a month (5:47, 6:03). She gave metrics, not a target — reasonably, because there is no baseline to beat.

The first objective is not a number. It is the first measured ratio of any size.

One project. One campaign, run end-to-end. Exposure tagged, donations attributed, and a real fraction at the end of it — even if it is three donors on eight hundred impressions. That fraction becomes the denominator every later campaign is judged against, and producing it forces exactly the four things she says are missing: a defined audience, a single message, a tagged asset, and a donation path that remembers where the donor came from.

The Shakespeare Steps is the natural candidate if it turns out to be a CITYarts project, because she has already thought about its two halves. If it is not, the same shape works on whichever project is next to break ground.
