Every price below was read off the live store this afternoon by parsing the product pages, not copied from a review site. The 3D printing web is thick with pages that restate each other and get a build volume wrong on the way through.

One hotend is a deliberate subtraction, not a compromise. The AMS still gives you four colours; what you give up is dissolvable support material and purge-free swapping, and neither matters for functional parts — which is what a garage makes. Tom's Hardware titled its H2S review "The One We Wanted" for exactly that reason.

"Everything you print auto goes to China"

Peter is describing a real default and calling it a property. It is a default.

Out of the box the printer registers to a Bambu account and jobs, models, and the chamber camera route through their servers. LAN-only mode ends that: the slicer talks to the printer directly over the local network, no print data touches Bambu, and the camera stays local too. It is one toggle on the touchscreen. Slicing was always local anyway.

What it costs: registration still needs a one-time cloud account, firmware and diagnostic checks still call home, and you lose watching a print from your phone at the office. What it does not do is make the firmware auditable — so the honest statement is that this is a managed constraint, not a guarantee. If provable isolation were the requirement, the answer would be a Prusa and a different budget.

Bambu tried to close this hatch once, in January 2025, and reversed after the community revolt. Worth knowing: the escape exists at the vendor's discretion, and they have reached for it.

Three things to settle, none of which block the purchase

  1. Does the garage drop below 10 °C in winter? Below that, these printers throw calibration errors and the filament system stops connecting — a hard failure, not a quality gradient. A $15 logging thermometer on the bench for a week answers it, and it changes where the machine lives, not which machine it is.
  2. What do you want to model in? Onshape, Fusion, FreeCAD, OpenSCAD. This becomes the real bottleneck about a week after delivery, and you draw for a living, which changes the answer.
  3. Does the house network have a guest or IoT VLAN? If so the printer goes on it and the sovereignty question closes properly.

What needs you

  1. Buy it, or tell me to buy it. H2S + AMS 2 Pro, ~$1,900 with the day-one kit. Money is yours; everything after the payment — unboxing, LAN mode, network, profiles, first prints — is mine.
  2. If you want laser cutting, say so now. That is the H2D and $500 more, and it is the only answer that changes the machine.
