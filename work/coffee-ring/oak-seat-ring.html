The one thing not measured

The dripper is a Hario V60 ceramic 02 — identified from the video, and the 60° cone plus Hario's published W140×D120×H102 mm fix its whole profile. What Hario do not publish, and what this part actually touches, is the flat base plate it stands on. 4.094, his caliper, 2 September — 104 mm. For three weeks it was 3.86 by photogrammetry off three video frames, with a stated spread of 0.24 that did not contain the answer; the last number in the part to meet a caliper, and it moved.

It no longer sets the flange, and the sentence that used to live here still said it did: ∅flange = plate + 2×(flat + roundover), which at these numbers computes to 4.54 and not 4.00. That formula described Mk4. From Mk5 the flange is set by the STEEL — exactly the pitcher’s 4.00 rim — and what the plate decides now is only how much oak shows around it: -0.087″ of flat each side at 4.09, and +0.122″ proud at the top of the spread. The load goes through the funnel’s foot collar, not the plate edge, so a wrong number here is visible rather than structural. Ten seconds with a caliper still settles it — and no amount of processing recovers what the backlit exposure destroyed, see docs/trials.md T5.

Hold the blank by the flange face, bore through to ∅2.52, then turn the spigot side and the outer diameter. Reverse onto a jam chuck or an expanding mandrel in that bore to face the flange side. The bore is the datum in both setups, which is why it is cut first — every dimension on the drawing above is referenced from it, so an error there is the only one that compounds.

The 90° internal corner where the spigot meets the flange underside is the one place a round-nosed tool will leave a fillet it should not. A square-ended scraper or a skew gets it clean; a fillet there is cosmetic, not functional — nothing seats against that corner.

Turn at 7–9 % moisture content. The clearances above are derived from a 4 % swing about that, which is the indoor range this part lives in. Turned wet, at 14 %, the spigot loses more than the whole 0.09 on the way down to equilibrium and the ring will not come off the pitcher. This is the one process condition that cannot be recovered by measuring more carefully.

Turning it

The part is a surface of revolution, so the profile above is the whole specification. Face and bore to ∅2.52 first — every other dimension is referenced from it. Turn the ∅4.00 flange, then step down to the ∅3.41 spigot at 0.69. Chamfer both bore mouths 45° by 0.06, round the outer edges R0.04, part off at 1.24. The flange face is left flat edge to edge — no recess, nothing for coffee to sit in. Roughly 83 g finished in live oak, 64 g in white oak — live oak is a third denser than the tables most people have in mind, and it twists; see docs/trials.md T6.

Every number in the part has now met a caliper. The last two — the funnel's foot drop and its plate — were read on 2 September: 10 mm and 104 mm. The first doubled the hedged value the height hung on and moved the part; the second only changed what shows.
