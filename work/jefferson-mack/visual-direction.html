Visual direction · jeffersonmack.art · 2026-08-31

Anvil

The page is the anvil face. The work is the only thing on it that catches light. One token set, two designed schemes — near‑black by default because that is the ground blackened steel needs, warm paper for reading.

This page shows the recommendation. The argument is in docs/visual-direction.md. Nothing here is decorative: no gradient, no drop shadow, no rounded corner, no scroll animation — the page obeys the anti‑slop rules it is arguing for.

Derived from the metal, not from a mood board

02 — Type, set in real type

Fraunces · Archivo · IBM Plex Mono

The tension has to sit between the photograph and the page, not inside each of them. The photograph carries the hammered half. The type carries the contemporary half. That rules out every forge‑flavoured typeface: if the lettering is also hammered, the tension collapses into a theme restaurant.

Fraunces · variable · opsz 9–144 · SOFT 20 · WONK 1 · display only

An antique from the future

Fraunces is a variable revival of nineteenth‑century display type with SOFT and WONK axes — an antique letterform that can only exist because of a modern font format. It is his thesis, in type. Its real optical-size axis means captions and headlines are drawn differently rather than scaled, which is the lesson measured off White Cube's three cuts of Beausite.

Fraunces · 1.9rem · piece titles

Wall piece, blackened mild steel

Archivo · 400 · one weight for running text

A grotesque drawn for signage and print — American industrial vernacular, no costume. One weight, so hierarchy has to be built from size, position and space rather than from boldness. Bold text is the cheapest hierarchy there is, and it is the first thing that makes a page look generated. Michael Anastassiades ships his entire site in one weight of one family; the restraint is the point.

Archivo · 0.8125rem · captions and secondary text, set in --sand.

IBM Plex Mono · 400 · every number, dimension and label

MILD STEEL · BLACKENED, WIRE-BRUSHED, OILED

Dimensions in mono read as a specification. That is his own argument, point four: he is selling legible making to people surrounded by objects whose workings cannot be read.

03 — Piece detail, mocked

Never alone. Always walked into.

Two of his own lines govern this layout, and both of them overturned an earlier draft. “These need to be in a gallery because they give themselves context; one of them doesn't.” — so a piece is introduced in adjacency, never as an isolated hero. “It's not even three different images… you just slide your finger up and it's going to zoom right in.” — so the deep view is a continuous zoom, not a sequence of frames and not a lightbox.

Slot 3 is the dimension line above. An empty labelled slot is correct; an omitted slot is not. A missing scale cue has to be visible as a hole so that it gets filled.

Jefferson on this piece · 1:40 · arms on scroll, never overlays the work

Provisional. The audio-guide idea and the phrase “not obscuring the thing” are Dmitry's, proposed to Jefferson on tape — not Jefferson's request. Built as a slot until his assent is located at source.

Note what is absent. No drop shadow under any image — depth comes from four dark values in the ground and from the raking light inside the photograph, never from CSS. No rounded corners. No hover‑scale. No fade‑up on scroll. No parallax. No “view gallery” lightbox. Each of those substitutes an interface event for the walk toward an object, and the walk is the entire brief.

04 — The two directions not recommended

Kept, because parts of them ship

These are not three rival tastes. Direction 2's warm paper became Anvil's light scheme; Direction 3's specification block became the piece-detail component above. What is shown here is what remains distinct.
