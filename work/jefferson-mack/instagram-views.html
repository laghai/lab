Read this before the charts

Likes exist on all 345 posts. Views exist on 7. Instagram only publishes a view count on video — 320 of Jefferson's posts are photographs, and a photo has no public views number at all, not a zero. Of his 25 video and carousel posts, only 7 return a count; the feed videos from 2016–2018 predate the metric.

So likes are the only metric that spans the whole library, and the charts below lead with them. "Total views" means views across the seven posts that have the number — it is not how many people have seen his work. The last section says where the real number lives.

So how does Jefferson track his views?

His account is already a professional/business account, which matters — it means the real numbers are available to him and simply are not being looked at.

  1. The public number only exists on video

    What is on this page is everything the outside world can see. If he wants views on photographs, no amount of scraping will produce it, because the number is not published.

  2. Instagram Insights has the number that is missing here

    Because he is on a professional account, every post — photos included — has reach and impressions in Insights, under "View insights" on each post and in aggregate in the Professional Dashboard. That is the metric that answers his real question, and it covers all 345 posts rather than 7.

  3. The finding is about format, not effort

    Twenty video posts out of 345 produced every view he has, three times the likes per post, and his single best post ever. The one reel that reached non-followers out-reached his entire follower list by eleven times. His photographs reach the 1,716 people who already know him; his videos reach people who do not.

  4. Engagement fell while the audience grew

    2016 to 2018 averaged around 80 likes a post. 2023 to 2025 averages 24 to 35. The account did not lose its audience — it stopped posting for three years, and came back mostly in photographs.
