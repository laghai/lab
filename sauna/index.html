Locking the scale against the framing

A LiDAR scan has no external ruler — but the building already contains one. Framing is laid out on 16 or 24 inch centres, held to a fraction of an inch by the tape the framer used. If the scan says the joists are 15.7 inches apart, the scan is 1.9 % short and every dimension it produced is short with it.

A cloud job sweeps a projection direction through 180°, histograms the points at quarter-inch bins, and scores the periodicity by autocorrelation. Real framing gives a sharp comb; everything else smears. The result becomes a scale factor applied to every number on this page. It refuses rather than guesses: a weak comb, or a period that is not near a nominal value, returns no correction.

Result: it did not work, and the way it failed is the useful part. Six bands across the two scans all returned a period of 9.88 – 10.15 inches — but at projection angles of 25°, 52°, 140°, 152°, 153° and 162°. Real joists are parallel: they would pick the same angle every time. A periodicity that is identical in every direction is not a row of members, it is an isotropic lattice — the scan's own mesh reconstruction grid. The comb RMS of 1.5–1.8 inches settles it; a framer holds a quarter inch.

So no scale factor was derived, and none has been applied. The scan's absolute scale remains unverified, which raises the value of the tape measure considerably: three hand measurements on site now settle what no amount of cloud compute could.

Why the heater really has to go in the floor

The owner wanted the niche for head height. At an 85-inch ceiling he does not need it for that — but he was right anyway, for a different reason. The bench arithmetic puts the top of the stones at 19.5 inches above the floor. A HUUM HIVE Mini is 29.5 inches tall. Standing on the floor it forces the upper bench up to 50.5 inches and leaves 34.5 inches of headroom — you hit your head.

And of ten manufacturers' manuals read, only HUUM's HIVE permits floor embedding — to 400 mm, with their own flange. Every other manual mandates a minimum floor clearance. The owner's ten inches sits inside that limit.

The heater

The ceiling height picks the heater, and the room size picks the kilowatts. Three manufacturers independently agree that 6 kW is illegal here.

The pick

HUUM HIVE Mini with the embedding flange and a UKU control. 170 kg of stone against a Harvia Cilindro's 90 and a Tylö Sense Elite's 20 — stone mass is what turns a ladle of water into sustained löyly instead of one hiss.

The circuit

240 V single phase, 33.3 A, #8 copper, 40 A breaker. Sized at 125 % continuous. The breaker can be the disconnect only if it is within sight of the heater — otherwise buy a lock-off.

No GFCI

Not required for a hardwired heater, and two manufacturers instruct against it — Harvia's manual says so verbatim. A resistive element in a humid box is a classic nuisance-trip source.

Two wrong citations, caught

Our own first pass built the spec on NEC Article 424 Part IX — which is radiant heating panels — and on 424.3(B), renumbered to 424.4(B) in 2023. The 2025 California Electrical Code contains zero occurrences of the word “sauna.”

Benches are a different purchase

Thicker, wider, clear grade only. Knots — not species — are the burn hazard: a knot holds heat and will take skin off. Never frame benches in thermally modified wood; it must not carry load.

Fasteners

304 stainless, driven from below so no metal touches skin. 316 costs four times as much and buys nothing in a chloride-free Sierra basement. 18-gauge brads — 16-gauge splits the tongue.

The wall build-up

Studs, mineral wool, aluminium foil vapour barrier with taped seams, an air gap on furring, then cedar. Never kraft-faced batt, never poly — it melts and off-gasses.

The doors — and the double-glazing question, answered

Single pane on both doors. Insulated sauna doors are real products, so the answer is not that the glass fails. It is that the insulated-glass industry certifies to a 60–80 °C service band while a hot-room ceiling zone runs 90–110 °C — above the peak of the qualification test. At 6,200 ft a unit sealed near sea level also arrives carrying about 3 psi outward before it is ever heated. The prize is roughly 230 W, about 4 % of the heater.

Telling detail: no all-glass double-glazed sauna door exists in the US at any price. Double glazing only appears in framed doors — because an insulated unit cannot take the through-bolts a frameless leaf needs without destroying its seal.

07

The build

Everything has to fit inside nine inches of floor. Delete the redundant bearing course and it does — the joists land straight on the pads.

  • No pressure-treated lumber anywhere in the building. Only concrete touches ground; the EPDM breaks the capillary path. That dissolves the treated-vs-hot-room conflict entirely.
  • Radon answered without a slab. A gravel pocket and a capped riser under the sealed liner — submembrane depressurisation, about $150 while the floor is open.
  • The heater well breaks the joist line and bears on its own pads at grade. It is deeper than the whole floor, so it cannot sit in a joist bay.
  • No floor drain. A 190 °F room evaporates a trap seal, and three weeks unused vents sewer gas into a sealed cedar box. Slope to the threshold, dry by ventilation, and set a capped sleeve now in case a shower ever follows.
  • Don't buy a framing nailer. The ceiling is fixed, so no wall can be tilted up — everything is toe-nailed in place, which is the job a framing nailer does worst. Buy an 18-gauge brad nailer and structural screws.

08

Permits

City of South Lake Tahoe. The ceiling-height risk that shadowed this whole project turned out not to exist — the City's own criteria sheet quotes the wrong rule.

TRPA: no jurisdiction

Interior remodelling is on its own exempt list. The $40k cap applies only to commercial and tourist structures. No notice, no application.

Ceiling: 6 ft 8 in, not 7 ft

CRC R313.1.1 — the 2025 code renumbered Chapter 3, and R305 is now termites. A sauna is not habitable space. 6 ft 4 in is allowed at beams and ducts. Our 85 inches clears it by 17.

≈ $476

At $12k valuation. But ≈ $1,080 if the electrician and the HVAC contractor each pull their own permit — agree that up front.

5 days, not 45

Plan check runs 0–17 days, median 5, measured off the City's own 2026 register. The FAQ claiming 45–60 is wrong. Basement egress is not required.

Keep new duct ≤ 25 feet

Above that the Energy Code forces diagnostic leakage testing, a certified rater and a registered compliance form. At or under: one unregistered form. Tell the HVAC contractor before he quotes.

Two risks to settle in writing

§150.2(b)1G reads as a prohibition on electric resistance as primary heat, and Climate Zone 16 is not among the exceptions. Both risks turn on whether a UL 875 dry-bath appliance is a “space-heating system.” No published interpretation exists.

The levers, cheapest first

The width — every foot is $300–450. Grade over species — thermo-spruce at $8.95/sq ft against clear cedar at $16–18 saves roughly $3,100 with no cost to the experience, if clear stock still goes on every surface skin touches. Shop the heater — the same HIVE Mini was $2,502 at one vendor and $4,326 at another on the same day. Rent tools instead of buying. And drop the fibre-optic lighting, which is 24 % of the accessories line.

10

What is still open

Nothing here is a guess dressed as a fact. These are the things that could still move the design.

  • Blocking  How far below the joists does the beam hang? The scan resolved the joist soffit strongly but never separated the beam from it, and the new framing ties flush to the beam. If it hangs below, the beam is the ceiling — not 95.7 inches — and the bench stack moves with it.
  • Blocking  The depths of the two rooms. The recording gives 6 ft and 7 ft across and nothing along. Bounded by the measured envelope, but not set by it.
  • Confirm  The 9-inch threshold. The scan says a strong horizontal plane sits 9.0 inches above the floor. It does not know what it is looking at. One tape measure settles the entire floor design.
  • Confirm  Is the shared house wall insulated? The scan photos suggest it already is, which would be good news — leaving it bare roughly doubles the required kilowatts.
  • Confirm  Panel capacity. An Article 220 load calculation on the existing service. Free to run, and it is the single largest cost risk in the project.
  • Decide  Is $20,000 a target or a ceiling? Which levers get pulled depends entirely on the answer.
  • Ask  Get the Energy Code classification in writing before framing — is a UL 875 dry-bath appliance a “space-heating system”? Two live risks hang on it and no published interpretation exists.
